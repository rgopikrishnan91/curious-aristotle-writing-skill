# Adversarial Self-Review

Assess the work against its stated contribution and study design. Answer applicable questions with evidence from the supplied material. Mark each item `supported`, `needs clarification`, `needs evidence`, or `not applicable`, with a reason. During delivery-only repair, report substantive issues separately for the author.

## Six Review Dimensions

### 1. Contribution

- What knowledge, capability, resource, or corrective evidence does the work provide?
- Is the contribution stated at the scale the evidence supports?
- Is its connection to a concrete research or practitioner problem clear?
- Are replications, negative results, descriptive audits, and incremental improvements assessed on their own terms?

### 2. Writing Clarity

- Can a knowledgeable reader recover the argument from paragraph-opening sentences?
- Does each section open with its core claim?
- Are consequential decisions explained and terminology consistent?
- Are empirical claims grounded locally and shorthand glossed as required?

### 3. Evidence Strength

- Does the evidence support the claim type, magnitude, scope, and uncertainty?
- Are comparators appropriate and conditions fair where comparison is part of the claim?
- Are failures, mixed outcomes, and relevant limitations represented?
- Do not require competitive benchmark performance when the contribution makes no such claim.

### 4. Evaluation Coverage

- Does the evaluation cover the stated research questions and design claims?
- Are the selected cases, metrics, and criteria appropriate to those claims?
- Are ablations, sensitivity checks, or qualitative validation needed for an actual unresolved inference?
- Do not demand every evaluation technique for every paper type.

### 5. Method Soundness

- Are assumptions and operating conditions explicit?
- Are there hidden defects, invalid proxies, or unaddressed confounders?
- Are benefits and costs assessed when the contribution claims a tradeoff?
- Are methodological limitations separated from prose problems?

### 6. Reproducibility

- Are materials, procedures, and decisions described sufficiently for the stated kind of work?
- Are availability and access constraints clear?
- Can the reported measurements or reasoning be checked independently?

## Claim-Evidence Mapping

For each major claim, record the exact claim, supporting evidence, scope, and status. Use the map as an audit artifact; in final prose, restate the relevant finding with its number and citation rather than pointing readers to this map.

## Stop Rule

Stop when each applicable issue is either addressed within the authorized scope or clearly flagged for the author. Do not repeat review indefinitely or invent experiments to eliminate every hypothetical rejection risk.
