# Anti-Patterns

Named traps. If you catch yourself doing any of these, stop and restructure.

**1. Incremental patching framing.** Presenting a naive baseline then describing your delta. Makes work look low-value even when it isn't. Frame the contribution as addressing a genuine gap.

**2. Copula avoidance.** Using "serves as," "features," "boasts," "presents" when you mean "is" or "has." Press-release language.

**3. Synonym cycling.** Rotating "developers… engineers… practitioners… builders" in one passage. Repeat the clearest word.

**4. Significance inflation.** "A paradigm shift" for a 2% improvement. State what happened; let readers judge.

**5. Hedge stacking.** "It could potentially perhaps be the case that..." Commit or cut.

**6. Chatbot artifacts in prose.** "In this section, we will explore..." or "Let's dive in." AI meta-narration.

**7. Vague attribution.** "Experts believe," "Studies show" without naming the expert, study, or finding. Cite specifically or drop.

**8. Template openings.** "In the rapidly evolving world of..." or "In this paper, we propose a novel..." Open with the domain problem.

**9. False concession.** "While X is impressive, Y remains a challenge." Both halves vague. Name what's impressive and name the challenge, or pick a side.

**10. Dramatic reframing.** "This is not a technical problem; it is a structural one." "It's not about coverage, it's about trust." LLMs love this construction. State the actual point directly instead of staging a false pivot.

**11. Uniform structure as AI tell.** Every paragraph same length, every sentence 15-25 words, every section same shape. Vary deliberately.
