# Pre-Writing Checklists

Answer these questions before drafting. They force backward reasoning: know what you're arguing before you write.

## Empirical Study (RQ-driven, case study design)

1. What phenomenon are we investigating and why does it matter to practitioners?
2. What specific gap in empirical evidence are we filling?
3. What are the RQs and what would each answer change about current practice?
4. What threats are inherent in our design and how do we mitigate them?
5. What is the unit of analysis and does every conclusion stay at that level?

## Audit / Benchmark Analysis (survey + filter + evaluate)

1. What is the population we're auditing and what standard are we auditing against?
2. What dimensions of comparison matter and why those?
3. What would a "passing" result look like, and what does failure imply?
4. How do we ensure the audit criteria are fair to all systems compared?

## Catalogue / Taxonomy (literature-driven, challenge enumeration)

1. What is the organizing principle for the catalogue?
2. What evidence base supports each entry?
3. What does the structured view reveal that a flat list wouldn't?
4. Are the categories mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive?

## Position / Vision Paper (argument-driven, forward-looking)

1. What is the core argument in one sentence?
2. What evidence from practice or literature supports it?
3. What is the strongest counterargument and how do we address it?
4. What concrete action should the reader take after reading?

## Tool / Approach Paper (problem + approach + evaluation)

1. What specific pain point does this tool address and who feels it?
2. How does it work at the level a practitioner needs?
3. How do we know it works (evaluation against what baseline)?
4. What are the tool's boundaries and failure modes?
