# Wording Diagnostics

Shorten wording only when meaning, technical terminology, modality, and emphasis stay fixed. Use these candidates where they fit; do not replace words on sight or treat them as evidence of AI authorship.

## Meaning-Preserving Candidates

| Wording | Candidate | Check before changing |
|---|---|---|
| utilize | use | Same action |
| in order to | to | Same purpose |
| due to the fact that | because | Causal meaning already present |
| in the event that | if | Same condition |
| despite the fact that | although | Same concession |
| is able to | can | Same ability and conditions |
| conducted an investigation of | investigated | Same actor, action, and scope |
| serves as | is | Identity, not a distinct role or temporary use |

## Words That Need Context

- Reject "robust" as a general compliment. Replace it with the specific supported property, tested condition, or result; do not merely substitute "strong" or "reliable." Keep "robust" only in an established technical name, such as "robust regression," or a precisely defined robustness claim with the relevant evidence. Flag missing evidence rather than inventing a measurement.
- Reject abstract labels such as "this is structural," "a structural issue," or "fundamentally a systems problem" when they replace a concrete explanation. State which component, dependency, or constraint produces which consequence. Keep technical uses such as "structural typing" when that is the exact concept.
- Preserve precise technical uses of "significant," "leverage," "paradigm," and "novel"; remove promotional uses rather than swapping in approximate synonyms.
- For promotional uses of "robust," "comprehensive," "seamless," "pivotal," or "impactful," state the supported property, coverage, effect, or consequence. If the source supplies none, flag the unsupported claim.
- Do not turn "cutting-edge" into "latest" without support; both assert a comparison. Do not turn "crucial" into "must"; importance and necessity are different claims.
- Define the scope of "complete" or "comprehensive" when it is an intended coverage claim; do not silently weaken it to "thorough."
- Preserve "may," "can," "must," "approximately," and other words that bound certainty, necessity, or precision.

## Redundancy Checks

Remove paired words only when they express the same content in context. "Full and complete" can often become "complete." "Large in size" can often become "large." Keep repetition that distinguishes technical concepts or carries necessary emphasis.

Check fillers such as "basically," "really," or "actually" for a concrete function before cutting them. "Virtually" can express approximation; deleting it can turn an approximate claim into an absolute one.
